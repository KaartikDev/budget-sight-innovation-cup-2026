# Digital pilot logbooks: competitor brief

**Research checked:** 27 September 2026  
**For:** PaperPlane product planning

## Market read

The market splits between full-service electronic logbooks that make digital records easier to keep, and conversion tools that remove the paper backlog. Established logbooks mostly treat “verification” as import validation, audit/reporting, or instructor signatures—not independent proof that a flight happened. The best opening for PaperPlane is to make digitization auditable and trustworthy while remaining easy to leave with a portable export.

## Four product opportunities for PaperPlane

1. **Make every imported value explainable.** Keep the original page/file, extracted value, confidence or validation state, and user correction together at flight and field level. Give pilots a side-by-side source-to-record review, including low-confidence and skipped-field queues.
2. **Treat import as a safe migration, not a one-shot upload.** Provide source presets plus flexible column mapping; deduplicate on review; support dry-run, resumable batches, rollback, and idempotent re-import. Make clean CSV export part of the same workflow.
3. **Build a durable evidence trail.** Persist who changed what and when, import source, verification criteria/result/reason, and any instructor signature. Distinguish “data checked,” “source matched,” and “instructor endorsed”; never imply an unmatched record is false or a matched record is independently proven.
4. **Turn records into pilot-ready answers.** Saved views for currency, checkride/job applications, and audit packets should show the exact entries and assumptions behind each total, then export a dated report with evidence links. Make jurisdiction/rule-set and incomplete data visible rather than silently calculating certainty.

**Product fit:** PaperPlane’s existing OCR review, CSV portability, and verification work align with opportunities 1–3. The clearest near-term edge is source-linked OCR review plus transparent, persistent verification evidence; mature competitors already set a high bar for platform coverage, reports, schedule imports, and broad logbook configuration.
