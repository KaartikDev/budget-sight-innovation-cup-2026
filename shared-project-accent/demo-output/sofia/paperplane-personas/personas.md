# PaperPlane audience personas

**Research snapshot:** 27 September 2026 · **Market lens:** U.S. pilot training, with global workforce context. These are research-informed hypotheses for pilot interviews and campaign tests, not demographic claims or validated user profiles.

## 1. Student pilot — “Make every lesson count”

- **Jobs:** Capture flights accurately; see progress toward solo, checkride, and currency; keep endorsements and training notes together.
- **Pains:** Handwritten records are easy to postpone or misread; unsure which experience requirements remain; instructor and student records can drift.
- **Triggers:** First solo preparation, new aircraft endorsement, checkride planning, or a lost / damaged paper logbook. FAA guidance makes logbook endorsements part of solo readiness, and solo endorsements are placed in the student’s logbook. ([FAA: student pilot training](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/become/student_training), [FAA: student certificates and endorsements](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/become/student_cert))
- **Objections:** “I already have a paper book”; “Will my instructor accept it?”; “I don’t want to spend lesson time fixing data.”
- **Message:** *Log the lesson once. See what you’ve done, what’s next, and keep the record yours.*
- **Campaign idea:** **First Solo, Fully Logged** — a short checklist and free guided logbook setup offered through flight-school student onboarding.

## 2. Career-track pilot — “Turn hours into a career-ready record”

- **Jobs:** Build qualifying experience across ratings and aircraft; monitor hour totals and currency; produce a clean, portable record for applications and milestones.
- **Pains:** Multiple hour categories and scattered records make totals hard to trust; legacy paper books take time to digitize; a hiring or checkride deadline exposes gaps.
- **Triggers:** Starting commercial training, instructing to build hours, applying for a role, or approaching ATP eligibility. FAA pathways include restricted ATP eligibility below 1,500 hours for some graduates; Boeing’s 2026 outlook forecasts 674,000 new commercial pilots globally through 2045. ([FAA: ATP training and reduced-experience pathways](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/training/atp), [Boeing: 2026 Pilot and Technician Outlook](https://www.boeing.com/commercial/market/pilot-technician-outlook))
- **Objections:** “Will the totals stand up to scrutiny?”; “Can I export my data if I switch?”; “I already use a mature logbook.”
- **Message:** *Know where every hour came from—and take your records with you.*
- **Campaign idea:** **Career Hours Checkup** — a downloadable milestone audit paired with a paper-logbook import clinic and portable CSV export demo.

## 3. Flight instructor — “Spend the debrief teaching”

- **Jobs:** Record instruction and endorsements; review student progress before each lesson; keep signed records easy to retrieve across learners.
- **Pains:** Re-entering flight details and chasing student records eats admin time; inconsistent entries complicate progress reviews; endorsement wording and history need care.
- **Triggers:** Growing student roster, student approaching solo/checkride, or a review that reveals missing or inconsistent entries. FAA guidance says instructors must sign the logbook of each person receiving flight or ground training and maintain endorsement records. ([FAA Aviation Instructor’s Handbook, Appendix C](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/aviation_instructors_handbook/15_aih_appendix_c.pdf))
- **Objections:** “I need control over what I endorse”; “Digital signatures and records must be dependable”; “I can’t add another admin tool.”
- **Message:** *Review the flight, confirm the record, and keep the endorsement trail clear.*
- **Campaign idea:** **Five-Minute Flight Debrief** — instructor-led demo showing how a reviewed flight entry becomes a student-owned, retrievable record.

## 4. Flight-school operator — “One reliable record across the training pipeline”

- **Jobs:** Keep student training records complete and findable; support instructor handoffs and school oversight; reduce paper handling without disrupting instruction.
- **Pains:** Records live in separate books, spreadsheets, and staff workflows; staff turnover makes continuity fragile; migration and adoption can interrupt training.
- **Triggers:** New cohort or campus, record-retention review, fleet / instructor growth, or a push to standardize training administration. FAA-approved Part 141 schools operate against prescribed standards for facilities, personnel, equipment, and curricula. ([FAA: Pilot Schools Information](https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/training/pilot_schools))
- **Objections:** “Will this fit our existing process and approved curriculum?”; “Can we migrate without losing source records?”; “Who owns access and exports?”
- **Message:** *Give each pilot a portable logbook while making records easier for your team to review and hand off.*
- **Campaign idea:** **Paper-to-Pilot Cohort** — a limited-pilot migration for one training cohort, with an instructor walkthrough, CSV import preview, and end-of-cohort completeness review.

## Research notes

- The FAA counted **345,495 student pilot certificates** among estimated active U.S. pilot certificates at 31 December 2024; the total rose from 316,470 in 2023. This indicates a sizable and recently growing student-pilot audience, not the number of active trainees or paying customers. ([FAA, *Air Traffic by the Numbers: FY2024*](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/by_the_numbers/air-traffic-by-the-numbers-FY2024.pdf))
- Boeing’s **2026–2045** commercial aviation forecast estimates **674,000 new pilots globally**. It is an industry forecast, not a guarantee of jobs or a measure of U.S. demand. ([Boeing, *Pilot and Technician Outlook 2026–2045*](https://www.boeing.com/commercial/market/pilot-technician-outlook))
- Product implications reflect PaperPlane’s documented capabilities: OCR review, currency tracking, CSV import/export, flight verification, and saved views. Validate each persona’s priority, terminology, and campaign concept with pilots, instructors, and school administrators before targeting or pricing decisions.
